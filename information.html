SPOTTED LANTERNFLY

WHAT IS A SPOTTED LANTERNFLY?

The spotted lanternfly (Lycorma delicatula) is an invasive species native to Vietnam and China that presents significant economic and ecological risks. It is established in at least 19 U.S. states and the District of Columbia, and has been detected in 15–21 states. It has also spread to Windsor, Ontario, South Korea, Japan, and parts of Eastern Europe. This species is considered highly problematic because it threatens agricultural and forestry operations by damaging vineyards, orchards, hardwood trees, and ornamental plants. Its feeding activity also has ecological consequences, as it feeds on 173 plant species, weakening trees and contributing to the growth of sooty mold. In addition, the spotted lanternfly spreads rapidly, increasing the urgency of management and mitigation efforts.
